Set up a tank for the steady solve

A Tank's level and temperature are states, and in a steady state neither is always determined by the rest of the model. Two choices settle them: whether the level is pinned at L0, the parameter pinned, and whether the temperature is held at T0, the keyword fixed_temperature. Loss of coolant explains why; this page shows the setup for each case.

using STREAMusing STREAM.Components: Tank, Pump, HeatExchanger, Orifice, Environment, StateMachineusing STREAM.Assemblies: inseriesusing STREAM.LocalLoss: discharge_cdusing ModelingToolkit: @named, mtkcompileusing OrdinaryDiffEq: Rodas5Pusing SteadyStateDiffEq: DynamicSS

A tank the loop flows through

Leave the level pinned, the default, and let the temperature follow the inflow with fixed_temperature=false. The steady temperature is then the temperature of what flows in, here a heater's outlet:

@named tank = Tank(; area=2.0, L0=3.0, ports=(out=0.0, back=2.0), T0=20.0,                   fixed_temperature=false)@named pump = Pump(; ṁ0=5.0)@named heater = HeatExchanger(45.0)@named loop = assembly(inseries(tank.out, pump, heater, tank.back), tank, pump, heater)sys = mtkcompile(loop)sol = solve_steady(sys)@assert sol[sys.tank.T] ≈ 45.0(level=sol[sys.tank.L], temperature=sol[sys.tank.T])
(level = 3.0, temperature = 45.000000000000064)

The tank also fixes the loop's absolute pressure through its surface, so the loop needs no other pressure anchor. Release the pin for the transient, overrides=[sys.tank.pinned => false].

A tank with no inflow

A sealed tank, or one on standby behind a shut valve, has nothing flowing in to set its temperature. Either hold the temperature, fixed_temperature=true, which the steady solve needs nothing more for, or skip the steady solve and start the transient from the declared state, releasing the pin in the operating point. Here a sealed pool is heated from outside for ten minutes:

Q, V = 1.0e5, 2.0 * 4.0                   # heat [W] and volume [m³]@named sealed = Tank(; area=2.0, L0=4.0, ports=(bottom=0.0,), T0=30.0, Q_ext=Q,                     fixed_temperature=false)@named shut = Orifice(; area=5e-4, cd=discharge_cd(:sharp))   # never opens@named room = Environment()@named closed = assembly(inseries(sealed.bottom, shut, room.port), sealed, shut, room)sys = mtkcompile(closed)sol = solve_transient(sys, [sys.sealed.pinned => false], range(0.0, 600.0; length=61))warming = Q * 600.0 / (ρ(H2O, 30.0) * V * cₚ(H2O, 30.0))@assert isapprox(sol[sys.sealed.T][end] - 30.0, warming; rtol=1e-2)(T_end=sol[sys.sealed.T][end], estimate=30.0 + warming)
(T_end = 31.80488545432132, estimate = 31.804360186902585)

A tank fed and drained at once

A tank with a feed and a drain that does not depend on its level has a level of its own: the one at which the drain passes the feed. Release the pin for the steady solve, in the operating point, and the solve finds it. A tank drained through a hole at its floor settles where the hole passes the feed, $ṁ = C_d a ρ \sqrt{2 g L}$:

ṁ_feed, a, cd = 2.0, 5.0e-4, discharge_cd(:sharp)@named pool = Tank(; area=0.2, L0=4.0, ports=(bottom=0.0, feed=0.0), T0=30.0)@named supply = Environment(; T=30.0)@named feed = Pump(; ṁ0=ṁ_feed)@named drain = Orifice(; area=a, cd, dp_linear=1e-3,                       machine=StateMachine(; initial_state=:OPEN, initial_time=-1.0))@named outside = Environment()connections = [inseries(supply.port, feed, pool.feed), inseries(pool.bottom, drain, outside.port)]@named fed = assembly(connections, pool, supply, feed, drain, outside)sys = mtkcompile(fed)sol = solve_steady(sys, [sys.pool.pinned => false]; solver=DynamicSS(Rodas5P()),                  abstol=1e-10, reltol=1e-10)L_settled = (ṁ_feed / (cd * a * ρ(H2O, 30.0)))^2 / (2G_EARTH)@assert isapprox(sol[sys.pool.L], L_settled; rtol=1e-4)(level=sol[sys.pool.L], expected=L_settled)
(level = 2.2148327627157927, expected = 2.214832718516339)

The drain opened before the run, initial_time=-1.0, so it is fully open in the steady state. DynamicSS integrates to where the level settles, which a Newton solve from L0 may not reach; Get a steady solve to converge has more on it. It stops when the state has nearly stopped changing, and a level that approaches its steady value slowly is still a little off then, so the tolerances are tighter than the default.

Two tanks

Two tanks joined by a loop, with nothing leaving it, have flows that balance whatever water shifts from one to the other. Pin one of them and leave the other free, as one pressure is fixed in a closed loop of pipes. The pool and decay tank tutorial pins the pool and lets the decay tank find its level.
