Compute safety margins

threshold_analysis applies the thermal-hydraulic limits to one channel of a solution. What each limit means is in Thermal-hydraulic limits, and how to read the numbers in Margins.

The examples use a plate cooled on both faces by one channel, as in A fuel plate between two channels.

using STREAMusing STREAM.Thresholdsusing STREAM.Components: Pump, HeatExchanger, HeatDiffusion, Slab, ChannelAndContactsusing STREAM.Assemblies: inseries, symmetric_plateusing ModelingToolkit: @named, mtkcompilen = 10geometry = Duct(0.6, Rectangular(0.067, 0.0024); heated=0.063)plate = Slab(; x=range(0.0, 0.00127; length=3), z=range(0.0, 0.6; length=n + 1), y=0.063,             material=Solid(2700.0, 900.0, 180.0))@named fuel = HeatDiffusion(plate; power=3.0e4)@named ch = ChannelAndContacts(; n, geometry, g=-G_EARTH)@named cell = symmetric_plate(ch, fuel)@named pump = Pump(; ṁ0=0.4)@named hx = HeatExchanger(40.0)@named loop = assembly([inseries(pump, hx, cell.ch, pump), pump.outlet.p ~ 1.7e5],                       pump, hx, cell)sys = mtkcompile(loop)sol = solve_steady(sys)

Margins of a steady state

Name each margin you want and give the function that computes it from a ChannelState, the channel's solved state. The correlations take one directly. Arrange each so that larger is safer:

m = threshold_analysis(sol, sys.cell.ch;    onb = s -> bergles_rohsenow_t_onb(s) .- s.T_wall,      # K to the onset of boiling    chfr = chfr(q_CHF_sudo_kaminaga),                     # CHF over the local flux    ofi = s -> q_OFI_whittle_forgan(s) / 3.0e4,           # OFI power over channel power    twall = s -> 150.0 .- twall_limit(s; inhomogeneity_factor=1.2),  # K to a 150 °C limit)worst_case(m.chfr)
(value = 4.110012713190324, cell = 10, time = nothing)

The heat fluxes and the geometric correlations read the channel's own duct. Per-cell margins come back as vectors and channel-wide ones, like the OFI ratio, as numbers. worst_case gives the smallest value and its cell.

Choose the face

A channel between two plates has a heat flux on each face. chfr and the flux-based correlations take a face: one of the channel's faces, :left or :right here, or :max, the larger flux and the default. Face-by-face margins:

m = threshold_analysis(sol, sys.cell.ch;    left = chfr(q_CHF_mirshak; face=:left),    right = chfr(q_CHF_mirshak; face=:right))(minimum(m.left), minimum(m.right))
(9.791420097087483, 9.791420097087483)

Margins over a transient

Pass a transient solution, and each margin is evaluated at every saved time. A per-cell margin becomes a matrix, cells by times, and worst_case with the times finds when:

sol_tr = solve_transient(sys, sol, 0.0:0.1:5.0; overrides=[sys.cell.fuel.power => 4.5e4])m = threshold_analysis(sol_tr, sys.cell.ch; chfr=chfr(q_CHF_sudo_kaminaga))size(m.chfr), worst_case(m.chfr; times=sol_tr.t)
((10, 51), (value = 2.7062668367358063, cell = 10, time = 4.0))

The minimum can only fall on a saved time. Save often enough to resolve the events that drive it.

Leave out what a correlation does not cover

The OSV and OFI correlations are for forced flow. In a loss of flow, near zero flow they stop meaning anything. Mask those times out of the search rather than dropping the margin:

forced = sol[sys.cell.ch.inlet.ṁ, :] .> 0.05 * design_ṁofi = m.ofi                                   # one value per saved timeworst_case(ifelse.(forced, ofi, Inf); times=sol.t)

Margins of a state from elsewhere

The correlations also take plain numbers, for a check by hand or for states computed outside STREAM:

q_CHF_mirshak(60.0, 115.0, 1.7e5, 3.0)   # bulk °C, saturation °C, pressure Pa, velocity m/s
4.0809036712373993e6

Factory that returns a CHF ratio (CHFR) closure, dividing by the heat flux on one face or the largest, with a guard for zero or negative flux.

Critical heat flux from the Mirshak correlation (Mirshak et al., 1959), for fast flows (v > 1.5 m/s):

Critical heat flux from the Sudo-Kaminaga correlation for plate-type fuel (Sudo and Kaminaga, 1993; Kaminaga et al., 1998).

Channel power at the onset of flow instability, from the Whittle-Forgan correlation (Whittle and Forgan, 1967) with Fabrèga's flow-dependent η (Fabrèga, 1971):
