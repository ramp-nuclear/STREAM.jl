Utilities

STREAM.Utilities — Module
STREAM.Utilities

Grid resampling and axial profile helpers, for moving per-cell data between meshes that do not line up.

rebin_extensive resamples amounts (power per cell, mass per cell) and preserves the total. rebin_intensive resamples per-cell values (temperature, heat flux) and preserves the value. Both treat the field as piecewise-constant over each source cell and integrate its overlap with each target cell.

cosine_shape builds an axial power shape. x_boundaries builds the cell boundaries across a clad plate. local_power_shift raises the hottest rows of a power shape and keeps its total.

None of these validate or normalize their inputs; negatives, zeros and NaNs pass through.

STREAM.Utilities.cosine_shape — Function
cosine_shape(x, ppf=π/2; xmax=nothing) -> Vector{Float64}

A cosine power profile over cells with boundaries x, integrated over each cell and normalised so the shares sum to 1.

The profile is cos(π(x - xmax)/L). The extrapolated length L is set by the power peaking factor ppf, the ratio of the profile's peak to its mean over x: with ℓ the span of x and h = πℓ/(2L), that ratio is h/sin(h), solved here for h. The peak sits in the middle of x unless xmax moves it, as for a partially inserted control rod.

Integrating each cell rather than sampling its centre keeps the total right on a coarse or graded mesh.

Arguments

  • x: increasing cell boundaries, length(x) = ncells + 1 [m]
  • ppf: power peaking factor in [1, π/2]. 1 is flat, and π/2 is a cosine reaching zero exactly at the ends.

Keywords

  • xmax: where the cosine peaks, the middle of x by default

Returns

Vector{Float64} of length length(x) - 1, one share per cell.

Throws

  • ArgumentError: if ppf is outside [1, π/2]

Examples

Four equal cells with a peaking factor of 1.4: the middle cells take the larger shares, and the shares sum to 1.

julia> s = Utilities.cosine_shape(range(0.0, 0.6; length=5), 1.4);julia> round.(s; digits=4), sum(s) ≈ 1([0.1768, 0.3232, 0.3232, 0.1768], true)

STREAM.Utilities.local_power_shift — Function
local_power_shift(power, relative_shift=1.0, percentile=90.0) -> Array

A power shape with its hottest rows raised by relative_shift and the rest lowered to keep the total, for a local power uncertainty. This is Python STREAM's local_power_shift.

power is nz × nx, with z along the first axis, or a vector over z. The rows whose sum is at or above the percentile-th percentile of the row sums are multiplied by relative_shift, and every other row by the one factor that keeps the total. When every nonzero row is at or above that percentile there is nothing to take the power from, and the shape comes back unchanged; percentile = 0 always does this.

Arguments

  • power: the power per cell
  • relative_shift: the factor on the hottest rows (default 1.0, no shift)
  • percentile: which rows count as the hottest, from 0 to 100; 100 is the hottest row alone (default 90)

Returns

The shifted power, shaped like power.

Examples

The peak raised by 10%, the other cells lowered to make up for it:

julia> round.(Utilities.local_power_shift([1.0, 2.0, 3.0, 2.0], 1.1, 100); digits=6)4-element Vector{Float64}: 0.94 1.88 3.3 1.88

STREAM.Utilities.rebin_extensive — Method
rebin_extensive(v, n_out) -> Vector{Float64}
rebin_extensive(v, src_edges, tgt_edges) -> Vector{Float64}
rebin_extensive(M, (nz_out, nx_out)) -> Matrix{Float64}

Resample an extensive quantity (an amount per cell, such as power or mass) onto a new grid, preserving the total: sum(out) == sum(v).

Extensive means the value scales with cell size, so splitting one cell into two halves the value and merging two cells adds them.

The (v, n_out) and (M, target_shape) forms assume source and target cells uniformly tile the same domain. The (v, src_edges, tgt_edges) form takes explicit cell boundaries (length(v)+1 and n_out+1 increasing values), which may be non-uniform. The 2D form is separable: it rebins along z, then x.

Inputs are trusted: nothing checks their sign, finiteness, normalization or shape.

Arguments

  • v, M: the per-cell amounts
  • n_out, (nz_out, nx_out): the target cell counts
  • src_edges, tgt_edges: source and target cell boundaries

Returns

The amounts on the target grid.

Examples

julia> Utilities.rebin_extensive([10.0], 2)2-element Vector{Float64}: 5.0 5.0

STREAM.Utilities.rebin_intensive — Method
rebin_intensive(v, n_target) -> Vector{Float64}
rebin_intensive(v, src_edges, tgt_edges) -> Vector{Float64}
rebin_intensive(M, (nz_out, nx_out)) -> Matrix{Float64}

Resample an intensive quantity (a per-cell value, such as temperature or heat flux) onto a new grid, preserving the value rather than the total.

Intensive means the value does not depend on cell size, so splitting one cell into two copies the value and merging two cells averages them. A constant field stays constant under any regrid. Each target cell ends up holding the area-weighted average of the source values it covers.

The forms are those of rebin_extensive, and inputs are not validated either.

Arguments

  • v, M: the per-cell values
  • n_target, (nz_out, nx_out): the target cell counts
  • src_edges, tgt_edges: source and target cell boundaries

Returns

The values on the target grid.

Examples

julia> Utilities.rebin_intensive([3.0, 7.0], 1)1-element Vector{Float64}: 5.0

STREAM.Utilities.x_boundaries — Method
x_boundaries(clad_N, fuel_N, clad_w, meat_w) -> Vector

Cell boundaries across a clad plate, starting at zero: clad_N equal cells over each cladding layer of width clad_w, and fuel_N equal cells over the meat of width meat_w between them. With clad_N = 0 the plate is meat alone and clad_w is not used.

Arguments

  • clad_N: cells in each cladding layer
  • fuel_N: cells in the meat
  • clad_w: width of each cladding layer [m]
  • meat_w: width of the meat [m]

Returns

The 2clad_N + fuel_N + 1 boundaries, ready for Slab(; x=...).

Examples

One cell in each 0.38 mm cladding layer and three across 0.51 mm of meat, in millimetres:

julia> round.(1e3 .* Utilities.x_boundaries(1, 3, 0.38e-3, 0.51e-3); digits=3)6-element Vector{Float64}: 0.0 0.38 0.55 0.72 0.89 1.27

Model a clad plate or a fuel rod builds the matching material matrix and power shape.
