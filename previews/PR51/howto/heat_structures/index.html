Model a clad plate or a fuel rod

HeatDiffusion conducts heat through a body described by a Slab, for a plate, or a Cylinder, for a rod or an annulus. Each describes the mesh, what every cell is made of, and the contacts between cells. The equations are in Heat conduction in fuel.

using STREAMusing STREAM.Components: HeatDiffusion, Slab, Cylinder, ChannelAndContacts, Pump,                         HeatExchanger, ConvectiveBoundaryusing STREAM.Assemblies: inseries, symmetric_plate, faceusing STREAM.Utilities: cosine_shape, x_boundariesusing ModelingToolkit: @named, mtkcompile

A clad plate

A plate is fuel meat between two layers of cladding. x_boundaries places the cell boundaries through its thickness: here two cells in each 0.38 mm cladding layer and three in the 0.51 mm of meat. A matrix of Solids, one per cell, gives each cell its material:

n_clad, n_meat, n = 2, 3, 12nx = n_meat + 2n_cladx = x_boundaries(n_clad, n_meat, 0.38e-3, 0.51e-3)z = range(0.0, 0.6; length=n + 1)clad, meat = Solid(2700.0, 900.0, 180.0), Solid(3000.0, 800.0, 60.0)in_meat = permutedims(n_clad .< (1:nx) .<= n_clad + n_meat)     # 1 × nxmaterials = repeat(ifelse.(in_meat, meat, clad), n, 1)          # n × nx

The power goes into the meat only. power_shape gives every cell its share, and is used as given: zero in the cladding, and in the meat the axial shape split by each cell's width.

widths = in_meat .* permutedims(diff(x))shape = cosine_shape(z, 1.4) .* (widths ./ sum(widths))@assert sum(shape) ≈ 1

A contact conductance on the faces between meat and cladding, x_contacts, stands for an imperfect bond. It takes one value per face through the plate, Inf for perfect contact:

interfaces = permutedims(in.(1:nx+1, Ref((n_clad + 1, n_clad + n_meat + 1))))plate = Slab(; x, z, y=0.063, material=materials, x_contacts=ifelse.(interfaces, 2.0e5, Inf))@named fuel = HeatDiffusion(plate; power=3.0e4, power_shape=shape)

The plate then joins a channel as any other does. Between two channels:

geometry = Duct(0.6, Rectangular(0.067, 0.0024); heated=0.063)@named ch = ChannelAndContacts(; n, geometry, g=-G_EARTH)@named cell = symmetric_plate(ch, fuel)@named pump = Pump(; ṁ0=0.4)@named hx = HeatExchanger(40.0)@named loop = assembly([inseries(pump, hx, cell.ch, pump), pump.outlet.p ~ 1.7e5],                       pump, hx, cell)sys = mtkcompile(loop)sol = solve_steady(sys)T = [sol[sys.cell.fuel.T[i, j]] for i in 1:n, j in 1:nx](hottest_meat=maximum(T[:, vec(in_meat)]), hottest_wall=maximum(sol[sys.cell.ch.T_wall_left]))
(hottest_meat = 88.24550200963479, hottest_wall = 83.12838493290018)

Conduct along the plate as well

By default each axial slice conducts across the plate only. axial=true adds conduction along it, and two more port arrays, thermal_top and thermal_bottom, at the plate's ends. A port left unconnected is adiabatic, so the ends need nothing unless something cools them:

@named long_fuel = HeatDiffusion(plate; axial=true, power=3.0e4, power_shape=shape)

A fuel rod

A rod is a Cylinder whose radii start at zero. Its rings can be pellet and cladding, and the gas gap between them a contact conductance on the face between them, r_contacts:

R_p, R_c, n_rod = 4.1e-3, 4.75e-3, 10r = [range(0.0, R_p; length=9); range(R_p, R_c; length=4)[2:end]]pellet = permutedims(r[2:end] .<= R_p)gap = permutedims(r .== R_p)rod = Cylinder(; r, z=range(0.0, 1.0; length=n_rod + 1),               material=repeat(ifelse.(pellet, Solid(10_400.0, 300.0, 3.0),                                       Solid(6_500.0, 330.0, 16.0)), n_rod, 1),               r_contacts=ifelse.(gap, 5.0e3, Inf))rings = pellet .* permutedims(diff(r .^ 2))                  # volume of each pellet ringshape = cosine_shape(range(0.0, 1.0; length=n_rod + 1), 1.3) .* (rings ./ sum(rings))@named pin = HeatDiffusion(rod; power=2.0e4, power_shape=shape)

A solid rod has one port array, thermal_right, on its outer surface; its axis has none. Each port joins whatever cools that cell of the surface. Here every cell is cooled by a ConvectiveBoundary at a given heat transfer coefficient and coolant temperature; a coolant channel would join the same ports, with a geometry whose heated perimeter is the rod's circumference.

dz = 1.0 / n_rodfilms = [ConvectiveBoundary(; name=Symbol(:film, i), area=2π * R_c * dz) for i in 1:n_rod]cooling = [[f.h ~ 3.0e4 for f in films]; [f.T_fluid ~ 300.0 for f in films]]@named cooled = assembly([face(films, pin, :thermal_right); cooling], pin, films...)sys = mtkcompile(cooled)sol = solve_steady(sys)centre = [sol[sys.pin.T[i, 1]] for i in 1:n_rod]surface = [sol[sys.pin.T[i, length(r) - 1]] for i in 1:n_rod](hottest_centre=maximum(centre), hottest_clad=maximum(surface))
(hottest_centre = 1249.1323209269012, hottest_clad = 334.5908157266133)

All of the rod's power reaches the coolant:

Q = sum(sol[f.thermal.Q] for f in (getproperty(sys, Symbol(:film, i)) for i in 1:n_rod))@assert isapprox(Q, 2.0e4; rtol=1e-6)Q
19999.99999999987

An annulus, such as a tube of fuel cooled inside and out, is a Cylinder whose radii start above zero. It has a thermal_left port array on its inner wall as well.
