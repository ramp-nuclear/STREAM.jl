## Decisions Made

**VAL-02: Gravity-driven NC, not buoyancy-driven NC**

The original VAL-02 formula used buoyancy (`delta_rho * g * H`) as the NC driver, which gives ~40 Pa for this geometry. The actual simulated NC mdot was 0.241 kg/s, vs the buoyancy-formula estimate of 0.017 kg/s — a 14x discrepancy.

Root cause: the series topology with HeatExchanger resetting temperature each pass means there is no sustained temperature gradient around the loop. Instead, the channel's gravity term (`rho * g * L ~ 9700 Pa`) drives downward reversed flow after Flapper opens. This is ~250x larger than the buoyancy term.

Fix: VAL-02 now verifies (a) `mdot_nc` is in the gravity-scale range `(0.05, mdot_grav_max * 2.0)` where `mdot_grav_max` is the Blasius channel-friction upper bound for the gravity head, and (b) NC is stable (CV < 5% over last 10% of simulation).

**Channel Dt term revert**

Plan 01 added `(L/A)*Dt(inlet.mdot)` to `_channel_base_eqs` to give each channel hydraulic inductance. This works in series topologies (where the Inertia and channel mdot are the same variable). In parallel topologies with 3-way junctions, MTK cannot combine these into a single differential state — creating an over-determined system. Since the series bypass topology uses a standalone Inertia component for hydraulic inductance, the channel Dt term is unnecessary and harmful.

## Issues Encountered

**MTK parallel topology limitation confirmed:** ANY Channel component (with bidirectional `instream()` temperature equations) in a 3-way parallel junction creates an over-determined MTK system. The `fully_determined=false` flag compiles successfully but the resulting system has 28 equations / 27 unknowns and cannot be solved by `ODEProblem`. This is a fundamental MTK limitation, not a STREAM.jl bug.

Workaround: the series topology provides all required LOF behaviors (Flapper firing, flow reversal, energy balance, NC equilibrium) without requiring parallel paths.
